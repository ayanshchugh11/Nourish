What causes food waste?

Food waste happens at home, in schools, restaurants, supermarkets and throughout the food supply chain. Some common causes include:

🛒

Overbuying

Buying more food than needed can cause products to expire before they are used.

🍽️

Large Portions

Preparing or serving more food than people can eat often leads to leftovers being thrown away.

🌡️

Poor Storage

Incorrect storage and unsuitable temperatures can cause food to spoil faster.

📅

Date Confusion

People may throw away food because they misunderstand date labels. Always follow food-safety instructions.

🏪

Retail Waste

Supermarkets may discard food because of damaged packaging, appearance standards or unsold stock.

🏫

School Canteens

Uneaten lunches and oversized portions can create unnecessary food waste in schools.

UNDERSTANDING THE IMPACT

Why should we care about food waste?

Food waste affects much more than our plates. It creates environmental, social and economic problems across the world.

🌍

Environmental Effects

Wasted food also wastes the land, water and energy used to produce it. When food decomposes in landfills, it can release methane, a powerful greenhouse gas.

❤️

Social Effects

Large quantities of food are wasted while millions of people face hunger and food insecurity. Better food management can support more responsible food systems.

💰

Economic Effects

Throwing away food means wasting the money spent on growing, transporting, purchasing and preparing it. This affects families and businesses.

GLOBAL GOALS FOR A BETTER FUTURE

Food Waste and the SDGs.

The United Nations Sustainable Development Goals are a global plan to create a fairer, healthier and more sustainable world. Reducing food waste directly supports two important goals.

02

SDG 2 – Zero Hunger

SDG 2 aims to end hunger, achieve food security, improve nutrition and promote sustainable agriculture. Reducing food waste helps us use available food and resources more responsibly. It also supports the need for better and more sustainable food systems.

12

SDG 12 – Responsible Consumption and Production

SDG 12 encourages people and businesses to use resources responsibly and reduce waste. Target 12.3 specifically aims to halve per-person food waste at retail and consumer levels by 2030, while reducing food losses throughout production and supply chains.

Reducing food waste is not only an environmental responsibility. It is also an important step towards a world with more responsible consumption and less hunger.
BE PART OF THE SOLUTION

What can students do?

You do not need to be an expert to make a difference. Students can help reduce food waste through small, practical changes at school and at home.

01. Take smaller portions

Start with an amount you know you can finish. You can always take more if you are still hungry.

02. Plan your meals

Think about what you will eat before buying or preparing food.

03. Store food properly

Follow food-storage instructions and keep food in suitable conditions.

04. Save suitable leftovers

Store leftovers safely and use them in another meal when appropriate.

05. Start a school campaign

Create posters, presentations and awareness activities to encourage classmates to reduce food waste.

06. Measure school food waste

Work with teachers and canteen staff to track how much food is left uneaten and identify ways to reduce it.

07. Share surplus food safely

Support school-approved food-sharing programmes that follow proper food-safety rules.

08. Compost food scraps

Encourage composting of suitable fruit and vegetable scraps instead of sending them to landfill.

Don't waste food. Value every bite. 🌱

Start a food-waste challenge!

The 7-Day Challenge

For one week, try to take only the food you can finish, keep track of leftovers and encourage one other person to reduce food waste. At the end of the week, reflect on what changed.
